## Recommended connected argument

Broomhead and Lowe establish nonlinear responses followed by linear output estimation. Que and Belkin supply the closest theoretical treatment of coefficient-regularized RBF models. Saralajew and colleagues explain the readout mechanism that can separate the nearest component from the winning class. Bugmann and Radhakrishnan and colleagues supply geometric and limiting links to nearest-neighbour decisions. Srihari and colleagues establish that finite-scale identity of nearest and aggregate exponential-potential rules has an older analytical history. The present question then becomes specific: for a fixed query and a ridge-fitted RBF family, which continuous bandwidth–penalty regions preserve the nearest selected input’s label?

Fazaeli-Javan and Ghiasi-Shirazi explain training-sample influence in softmax output neurons, a complementary explanatory target. D’Agostino and colleagues, Wurzberger and Schwenker, and the two Noorizadegan studies show how learning or normalizing Gaussian representations changes geometry and scale sensitivity. Shibagaki and Ndiaye supply the direct parameter-path connection: their score/validation bounds make interval reasoning explicit. This progression supports the manuscript’s joint bandwidth/penalty certificate without invented claims that earlier work failed to solve its own objective.

## One justified addition

**Proposed two-sentence synthesis:** Srihari, White, and Snabb (1979) compared nearest-neighbour classification with a rule that sums exponential potentials within each class, deriving geometric conditions and an algorithm for identical decision surfaces. Their results connect nearest-sample and aggregate-response decisions at finite scale; the present study tests a fixed query after coefficient-ridge fitting and certifies its agreement throughout a bandwidth–penalty region.

The 1979 study uses two class sums of prescribed positive radial responses and tests surface identity throughout input space. The present model fits coefficients, allows signed coefficients and multiclass outputs, fixes the query, and varies bandwidth and ridge penalty. The 1979 body contains no coefficient-ridge fit or ridge-penalty path. Keep this distinction factual; no global novelty claim is needed.
